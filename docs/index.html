A day with Tribu

Household plans usually scatter across separate apps and chat threads. Here is how an ordinary day looks when everything shares one quiet place.

07:12 12:40 17:30 20:15 21:00
07:12 Before anyone puts on shoes

Who needs to be where today?

Before coffee is poured, the wall display shows Mia heading to school, Leo's nursery drop-off, and the dentist at 09:00. Open Tribu on a phone or laptop for the same day in one timeline, filterable by person. The Shared Home Display also supports high-contrast E-Ink frames.

Monochrome layout for E-Ink frames (shown in German, one of 24 UI languages).

12:40 Standing in the supermarket

No more texting from aisle four.

Open the shared shopping list on your phone while someone at home adds milk or eggs. Items group by aisle with clear icons and update live over WebSockets as you tap them into the basket. Shopping guide.

17:30 Before anyone gets hungry

Dinner is already on the plan.

Sheet-pan vegetables on Tuesday, pasta on Wednesday, family pizza on Friday. Your weekly meal plan sits next to your recipes, so missing ingredients go straight onto the shopping list, and today's meal can appear on the kitchen display. Meal planning guide.

20:15 Looking ahead to the week

One calendar, synced to your phones.

Once the house is quiet, check Thursday's brunch at Grandma's, Friday's plumber, and recurring lessons alongside automatic contact birthdays. Built-in CalDAV and CardDAV keep iPhone, iPad, and DAV-compatible Android clients in sync, with opt-in VTODO tasks. Phone sync guide.

In the evening, the wall display shifts to tomorrow's schedule (shown in German).

21:00 Wrapping up the day

Small routines, shared goals, quiet praise.

Tasks keep recurring chores visible with clear assignees and priorities. Approve stars toward a child's wish like ice cream, chip in together toward a shared Zoo trip, or send praise without stars. Optional areas can be hidden anytime.

Your family's data stays at home.

A family organizer knows where your children go to school, when everyone is out of the house, and who is visiting for a birthday. Unlike cloud family organizers, Tribu keeps that information on hardware you control.

In your own database

Calendars, tasks, shopping lists, contacts, and backups live in your own PostgreSQL database. There is no account with a third party, no subscription fee, and no lock-in.

Safe on a shared tablet

A kitchen display is paired as a read-only device with a revocable token, not a fake family member. The display route leaves out account settings, emails, user IDs, and calendar source URLs.

Clear about external calls

Household data stays on your server. Optional display weather fetches forecasts from Open-Meteo using only location coordinates, and admin-only update checks may contact GitHub for release metadata.

For the person who runs the server

Tribu runs with Docker Compose on a home server, NAS, mini PC, or Raspberry Pi. Open http://localhost:3000 after starting the stack; the first registered user becomes the family admin.

mkdir tribu && cd tribu
curl -LO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/itsDNNS/tribu/main/docker/docker-compose.yml
curl -LO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/itsDNNS/tribu/main/docker/.env.example
cp .env.example .env
# Fill in JWT_SECRET and POSTGRES_PASSWORD
docker compose up -d
name: tribu

services:
  postgres:
    image: postgres:16-alpine
    restart: unless-stopped
    environment:
      POSTGRES_DB: tribu
      POSTGRES_USER: tribu
      POSTGRES_PASSWORD: ${POSTGRES_PASSWORD}
    volumes:
      - tribu_pg_data:/var/lib/postgresql/data

  valkey:
    image: valkey/valkey:8-alpine
    restart: unless-stopped

  backend:
    image: ghcr.io/itsdnns/tribu-backend:latest
    restart: unless-stopped
    environment:
      DATABASE_URL: postgresql://tribu:${POSTGRES_PASSWORD}@postgres:5432/tribu
      REDIS_URL: redis://valkey:6379/0
      JWT_SECRET: ${JWT_SECRET}
      SECURE_COOKIES: ${SECURE_COOKIES:-auto}
      TZ: ${TZ:-}
    depends_on: [postgres, valkey]
    volumes:
      - tribu_backups:/backups

  frontend:
    image: ghcr.io/itsdnns/tribu-frontend:latest
    restart: unless-stopped
    environment:
      BACKEND_URL: http://backend:8000
    depends_on: [backend]
    ports: ["3000:3000"]

volumes:
  tribu_pg_data:
  tribu_backups:

Before exposing Tribu to the internet: put it behind an HTTPS reverse proxy, set SECURE_COOKIES=true, keep JWT_SECRET stable in your persisted .env, set TZ to your household timezone, and test backup and restore.

Trying it first: Tribu does not run a hosted public demo. After starting the stack locally, click Try demo on the login page to explore sample household data without saving anything to PostgreSQL.

Docker & Stack
Multi-arch GHCR images for amd64 and arm64. Next.js 16, React 19, FastAPI, PostgreSQL 16, Valkey 8, non-root containers.
CalDAV & CardDAV
Bidirectional calendar and contact sync for iPhone/iPad built-in apps and Android through DAV-compatible clients such as DAVx5. Separate opt-in VTODO task token.
Home Assistant
Ships tribu_package.yaml and dashboard-card.yaml for sensors, quick capture, and household automations.
Sign-in & Security
OpenID Connect SSO (presets for Authentik, Zitadel, Keycloak, generic OIDC), optional two-step TOTP sign-in, admin backups, and audit log.
API, Webhooks & MCP
Scoped API tokens, outbound webhooks with WEBHOOK_ALLOWED_HOSTS, and a built-in /api/mcp endpoint for local AI (Ollama, llama.cpp, LM Studio, Open WebUI; disable with MCP_ENABLED=false).
Languages & PWA
24 UI languages, 10 built-in themes, global search, web push notifications, and an installable PWA for phones, tablets, and foldables.

Common questions

Is Tribu free and open source?

Yes. Tribu is released under the MIT License with source code and multi-architecture container images published publicly on GitHub. There are no subscriptions or paid tiers.

What hardware do I need to self-host Tribu?

Tribu runs with Docker Compose on a home server, NAS, mini PC, or ARM board such as a Raspberry Pi. Published GHCR images support both amd64 and arm64 architectures alongside PostgreSQL 16 and Valkey 8.

Does Tribu sync with iPhone, iPad, and Android?

Yes. Tribu includes built-in CalDAV and CardDAV sync for iPhone and iPad Calendar and Contacts apps, and works on Android through DAV-compatible clients such as DAVx5. Bidirectional VTODO task sync is available as a separate opt-in token for iOS Reminders or Tasks.org and OpenTasks on Android.

Is there a hosted online demo I can try first?

Tribu does not run a public hosted demo server. Instead, after starting Tribu locally with Docker Compose, click Try demo on the login page to explore realistic sample household data without saving anything to the database.

How do backups and restores work?

Tribu mounts a dedicated backup volume in Docker Compose and provides admin backup tools together with documented backup and restore procedures in the GitHub Wiki so you can test recovery on your own storage or NAS.

How does Tribu connect to Home Assistant and local AI?

The repository ships a Home Assistant package and dashboard card for sensors and quick capture, plus outbound webhooks and scoped API tokens. Tribu also exposes a built-in Model Context Protocol (MCP) endpoint at /api/mcp designed first for local AI tools such as Ollama, LM Studio, and Open WebUI, and can be disabled with MCP_ENABLED=false.
